## 1. Experiment Update - 18/10/2026

The three EWC seeds are now complete. This report also includes seeds 6 and 7 for MAS and RWalk. I use only complete method/seed runs in the tables below; the other runs were still in progress when I exported the results.

The EWC results at budget 0.3 repeat across all three seeds. With 8 poisoned images out of 150, detection is 100% for reckless BrainWash and 95–100% for cautious BrainWash. None of the clean simulations alerts. Budget 0.1 is less consistent: seed 7 misses every simulation containing 8 poisoned images, while seeds 6 and 8 detect many of them. With only 2 poisoned images, detection remains at 0–1%.

The second seeds confirm the threshold problems seen with MAS and RWalk. MAS still misses many poisoned images. RWalk flags every clean simulation in both completed seeds, despite good sample ranking at the larger attack budget.

The ablations continue to support tensor gradient norms. The four additional features make little difference to ranking at budget 0.3, but help in some weaker-attack cases. Rank has more serious limitations than the first seed suggested. In EWC seed 8, it misses almost all fully poisoned simulations. In RWalk seed 7, it flags 73% of the clean simulations.

## 2. Experimental Setup

### 2.1 Completed Runs and Task Roles

| Component | Setting |
| --- | --- |
| Benchmark | Split CIFAR-100, ten tasks with ten classes each; fixed class order |
| Complete EWC runs | Seeds 6, 7 and 8 |
| Complete MAS and RWalk runs | Seeds 6 and 7 for each method |
| Source task | Task 1: fit StandardScaler and logistic regression |
| Calibration data | Clean validation and reserve images from Tasks 1 and 4 |
| Development check | Task 4 test split; diagnostics only |
| Final evaluation | Task 9, without detector fitting, recalibration or feature selection |
| Target attacks | Reckless/cautious BrainWash × L-infinity budgets 0.1/0.3 |
| Dataset simulations | 150 distinct originals per simulation; 100 simulations per fraction |
| Ablations | Nine fitted variants, including the full detector |
| Unsupervised comparison | Rank and historical-reference MMD |

Tasks are numbered from zero. Features are extracted before the defender learns the incoming task. The Task 9 defender has therefore learned Tasks 0–8, but not Task 9.

The class order stays fixed across seeds. These repeats change model training and attack generation; they do not provide new datasets or class orders.

I fit a separate detector for each CL method and seed. The source training set contains 3,000 real Task 1 images, each represented by its clean version as a negative and its reckless BrainWash version at budget 0.3 as a positive. This gives 6,000 training rows from 3,000 originals. Random perturbations are used for evaluation only.

Each task is split into 3,000 training, 500 validation, 1,000 reserve and 500 test originals. All versions of the same image stay in the same split. Sample evaluation uses the clean and poisoned versions of the 500 test images. The other Task 9 splits are not used for detector fitting or calibration.

I fixed `history_mad` after the seed 5 pilot reported on 04/10, before starting these runs. The Task 4 test results are diagnostic; they do not select a different rule. Source-only and historical-quantile cutoffs are retained as frozen comparisons.

### 2.2 Training and Attacks

| Component | Setting |
| --- | --- |
| Victim training | 100 epochs per learned task |
| Regularisation coefficients | EWC: 500,000; MAS: 10; RWalk: 1 |
| Historical inversion | 2,000 iterations; 128 samples per historical task |

Each method/seed detector is tested on all four target attacks without retraining. Three of these settings also change the attack configuration from the source training attack. They are different BrainWash configurations, not different attack families.

The attacker uses all incoming-task training originals to generate the perturbations, including those later assigned to the detector test split. These originals are held out from detector fitting, but are not unseen images for the attack optimisation.

### 2.3 Features

| Supervised feature | Dimensions | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parameter-tensor gradient norms | 80 | L2 norm of the loss gradient for each backbone weight or bias tensor |
| Entropy | 1 | Spread of the head's predicted class probabilities |
| Confidence | 1 | Largest predicted probability |
| Margin | 1 | Difference between the two largest predicted probabilities |
| Activation norm | 1 | L2 norm of the representation entering the classification head |

The full supervised detector has 84 inputs. Gradients use the model's predicted class as the loss target. True-class probability, loss and historical-gradient cosine features are not inputs to this detector. Image-class labels are used to construct the benchmark and stratified splits, but not for feature extraction or scoring.

The incoming head is initialised with a fixed seed and has not been trained on the incoming task. Its entropy and confidence are probe responses, not calibrated estimates of classification correctness. The model stays in evaluation mode during extraction, with no backbone update or change to BatchNorm state.

## 3. Frozen Thresholds

For each historical task, I score its clean validation images and calculate two cutoffs: a clean quantile cutoff and a robust cutoff obtained from the median and scaled median absolute deviation of the score logits, using a multiplier of three. I keep the larger cutoff, then take the maximum across Tasks 1 and 4.

I use the separate reserve-clean images to set the count threshold. For each task, I calculate a one-sided Clopper–Pearson upper bound on the sample alert probability. The larger historical bound determines the smallest count whose binomial upper tail is at most 0.025. The error budget for estimating the alert probability is also 0.025. A simulation alerts when its flagged-image count reaches this count threshold.

| EWC | 7 | 0.116538 | 4 | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| EWC | 8 | 0.190355 | 5 | 0.0% | 0.0% |

The rule is fixed across runs, but each fitted detector has its own numerical cutoffs. Those scores come from different fitted models, so their absolute thresholds should not be compared as if they were on a common probability scale. The historical count calculation is a heuristic under task shift, not a guarantee of the Task 9 false-positive rate.

## 7. Unsupervised Results

### 7.1 Rank and MMD Setup

This experiment uses no labelled clean/poison detector training examples and no known-clean Task 9 calibration data.

Both tests use ten descriptors: the global backbone gradient norm, five stage gradient norms, entropy, confidence, margin and activation norm. Each reference contains 128 inversion samples from each of Tasks 0–8, or 1,152 samples in total. The reference and incoming descriptors use the same Task 9 defender and fixed incoming head.

Rank makes a decision for the whole simulation, not for individual images. I calculate Kendall tau-a correlations between descriptor pairs and compare the resulting vector with each historical task separately. Historical references alone determine which pairs are retained: the centred jackknife pseudovalues must have variance above 10^-12 in every reference.

For each comparison, the statistic is the largest absolute correlation difference across the retained pairs. I use 499 Gaussian multiplier bootstrap draws from the centred jackknife pseudovalues to approximate its variation. The p-value is `(1 + number of bootstrap statistics at least as large as the observed statistic) / 500`. These draws do not generate new image datasets.

The final p-value is the maximum of the nine historical-task p-values. A simulation alerts only if all nine comparisons reject at 0.05. Failing to reject one comparison does not establish that the simulation is clean.

MMD is the earlier historical-reference baseline, using a random-Fourier approximation to an RBF-kernel two-sample comparison and a permutation test. It can respond to marginal feature changes as well as dependence changes. Rank focuses on pairwise rank dependence. Both tests compare real incoming images with synthetic historical references, so a difference is not necessarily caused by poisoning.

No simulation was skipped as unsupported. EWC's clean alert rates are 4%, 8% and 3% for seeds 6, 7 and 8. MAS gives 4% and 0%. RWalk gives 10% and 73%. The 73% clean alert rate makes RWalk seed 7 unusable for deciding whether a dataset is poisoned.

Small poisoning fractions remain difficult in all three EWC seeds. Seed 8 also exposes a failure at full contamination: detection is 0% for both reckless budgets and cautious budget 0.1, and 1% for cautious budget 0.3.

For example, EWC seed 8 reckless budget 0.3 is detected in 99% of simulations with 75 poisoned images, but in none with all 150 images poisoned. I inspected the individual historical comparisons: the fully poisoned simulations still have at least one comparison that does not reject, so the maximum-p-value rule does not alert. This explains the recorded decisions, but does not show that the distributions are equal.

Mixing clean and poisoned images can change dependence differently from using poisoned images alone. Rank also cannot detect strictly increasing marginal transformations that preserve its pairwise correlations. These limitations may explain the pattern, but I have not established which mechanism causes this particular failure.

MMD flags every reported clean, poisoned and random-control simulation. Rank avoids many of these alerts in EWC and MAS, but it does not provide reliable low-fraction detection or consistently control clean alerts across methods. Its approximate p-values are neither poisoning probabilities nor a guarantee of a 5% clean false-positive rate.
